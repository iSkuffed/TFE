# TFE Roadmap

The Fallen Eagle for EU5: a 395 AD start at the division of the Empire between Arcadius and Honorius. Inspired by the CK3 mod *The Fallen Eagle* (Workshop 2243307127, installed locally). We borrow its ideas in spirit, not wholesale.

Background research: `reports/Fallen Eagle ideas for EU5.md` (notes in `research_notes/Fallen Eagle ideas for EU5/`).

## Design rules

- **Decay has a face.** CK3 TFE retired its global "Imperial Competency" meter in August 2026 because players could not read it. Every movement of Unity (or any other collapse meter) must come from a named, visible cause. Every threshold unlocks something the player sees, not a silent penalty.
- **Pace the chaos.** Aim for about one usurpation or defection per decade per Augustus. Build cooldowns in from day one.
- **Characters become countries, estates and IO statuses.** Where CK3 TFE used a character or opinion score, use a country, an estate, an IO special status or a per-region script value. Where it used a struggle, use a situation.
- **Fun over accuracy.** Prefer playable fills and fun alt-history formables over empty gaps.
- **Beat TFE's weak spots.** Hosts carry their real warband (no spawned stacks). We own the map layer outright. EU5 pops already give us the religious and cultural minorities TFE had to bolt on.

- [x] The land in 395 (`tools/location_templates.py` writes `in_game/map_data/location_templates.txt`; rerun after a patch): Carthage and ten Proconsularis/Byzacena towns are Granaries of Rome (wheat, +75% wheat output, RGO expanded at start by `on_action/tfe_lands.txt`); Transdanubia and Slavonia are Pannonian Recruiting Grounds (+0.025 local manpower each, flat: locations have no base manpower to raise). As template modifiers they badge the goods marker in the raw-material map mode with their one effect's icon (soldiers with a plus: `modifier_icons/tfe_modifier_icons.txt`). Italy gave a fifth of its wheat land (9 of 44, all south of the Po) to the villas' vines, herds and flocks, so Rome lives on African grain. Italy's remaining mainland wheat yields less so African grain sells there: Latifundia (−40%) in the dole-fed centre and south, Annona Militaris (−20%) in the north, whose grain went in kind to the court and army. Sized so Italy's shortfall roughly matches the Granaries' +75%; Sicily and Sardinia untouched. The Roman and near-Roman world grows only what it grew in 395 (per-region tables in `location_templates.py`): no silk, sugar, saltpeter or coal; rice, cotton and saffron only where there is ancient evidence; medieval mines, cloth and wool gone; Roman specialities in (Tyrian purple, Belus glass sand, Baetican oil, Noric iron, Corycian saffron).
- [x] Frontier works (`building_types/tfe_frontier.txt`, placed by `borders.py` ROMAN_FRONTIER): Hadrian's Wall at Newcastle and Hexham, the Limes on the Rhine, in Raetia and Noricum, on the ripa Pannonica and the lower Danube. Fort level 1 with a zone of control, +fort defense, never built anew. First step on #13.
- [x] Eastern Europe in 395 (`tools/overrides/70_eastern_europe.txt`, research in `reports/Eastern Europe in 395.md`): the forest and the Baltic are no longer empty land the Huns must colonise. 22 tribal countries: the Lugii in Poland, the Antes, Balts from the Sudovians to the Golyad around Moscow, the Finnic and Volga peoples from the Estonians and Ves to the Mordvins, Mari, Udmurts and Permians, the Imenkovo farmers on the middle Volga, the Ural Ugrians, the Carpi, Suomi and Korela. The Venedi take Lesser Poland, the Quadi western Slovakia, the Huns the steppe fringe. The Antes, Carpi, Mordvins, Imenkovo and Ural Ugrians start as Hunnic tributaries. Only the Antes' and Carpi's Hunnic ties are sourced (Jordanes, Zosimus); the rest are attested peoples with invented polities. Medieval towns there (Moscow, Kazan, Riga, Novgorod) are villages again. The far north (Sami lands, Kola, Arkhangelsk, White Karelia) stays empty.
- [x] The Hunnic Storm (#10, `situations/tfe_hunnic_storm.txt`): a three-phase situation from day one. The Huns lead the Hunnic Yoke (`international_organizations/tfe_hunnic_yoke.txt`: their five tributaries of 395, each paying ~1% of the members' income as tribute), bring neighbours under it (Submit to the Yoke) and raid Rome for a place in the Yoke (Grand Raid: the loser joins and pays the tribute; the Imperium Romanum loses Unity while it does). The five starting tributaries are also proper `tributary` subjects of the Huns. A Yoke of 8 with a paying Rome (or 6 from 434) opens the Scourge: the Huns' ruler may take the Scourge of God trait, outsiders get Stand Against the Scourge, and the Germanic hosts beside the Yoke are asked to take the road west. The Scourge's death opens the Reckoning: the members may rise (Break the Yoke), and in 10 years the Yoke either breaks (Nedao) or the Huns endure.
- [x] The Theodosian Walls (`building_types/tfe_theodosian_walls.txt`, `script/theodosian_events.py`): the Theodosian Walls are locked out of the buildings tab until 1 Jan 405 (a `REPLACE:` of vanilla's block, guarded by `tools/test_vanilla_copies.py`), when "A Wall Across the Peninsula" offers them to Constantinople's owner: start them now at a third off, or refuse and build them by hand at full price. Arcadius's stats are rolled at game start (`tfe_arcadius.1`, weighted around a pious, guided emperor: adm/dip 30-50, mil 10-30), and Arcadius, Honorius and Placidia share one face shape (`tfe_historical_chr.txt`).

## Tier 1: make 395 play like TFE's opening

1. **Foederati subject type.** ✅ v1 (`subject_types/tfe_foederati.txt`): no tribute, the foederati join all of the overlord's wars, and Rome pays them the *annona* (2 gold/month). The foedus lapses when either ruler dies ("The Oath Is Dead": swear anew, or take what was promised = independence plus a 5-year Broken Foedus CB). Visigoths and Salihids start as EAR foederati sworn to Theodosius, so both choose on day one (the AI's Goths revolt 20:1). Only an Augustus can strike a foedus, through ordinary diplomacy. Still to do: a host that settles on Roman land is offered a foedus (*Hospitalitas*); a Unity cost for lapsed foedera; tune the annona against real incomes.
2. **Senior Augustus and edicts.** ✅ v1. The Senior Augustus is an IO special status (`international_organization_special_statuses/tfe_roman_empire.txt`) that makes its holder the IO's leader country. Arcadius holds it at start; when the Senior dies it passes to the other Augustus. The Senior decrees one of five edicts from the IO's Laws tab (`laws/tfe_edicts.txt`): Fide, Codificatio, Cursus Publicus, Agri Deserti or Annona. Each has one benefit and one cost for every member, needs Unity ≥ 50, spends 5 Unity, and binds for 5 years before it can be replaced. Still to do: a Caesar status; a way for the junior Augustus to act (claim seniority on the union `take_over_seniority` model, or petition an edict); the AI decreeing edicts well; a "Senior status contested" Unity driver (#4).
3. **A 395 opening that forks on named people.** ✅ v1 (`events/tfe_opening.txt`). Stilicho's Regency (army and nobles) and Rufinus's Prefecture (rich but resented) are country modifiers. Day one: Stilicho sends the Eastern legions home (+5 Unity, and +5 more when Rufinus falls) or keeps them (four footmen and two armored horsemen at Milan for free, −15 Unity, and the East remembers). 27 Nov 395: Rufinus is murdered, and the East picks Eutropius's chamber or Arcadius alone. Gildo's Africa breaks away as a kingdom once his hold reaches 100, or by autumn 397 at the latest (#25); the East may take it as a vassal (−15 Unity), and if the West sends Mascezel, the Augusti can end up at war. Early 407: Constantine III's Britain becomes a rebel empire; the West can denounce him or recognise him. Usurpers and Augusti get Suppress the Usurper / Claim the Purple CBs for 10 years (`casus_belli/tfe_usurpers.txt`). Alaric's revolt is the foedus event (#1). Two buttons for the West in the Imperium Romanum window (`generic_actions/tfe_roman_empire.txt`): Support Stilicho's Claims (Unity −10, a 10-year Claim Eastern Illyricum casus belli on eight Illyrian provinces) and Align the Visigoths (Unity −10, buys Alaric out of the Eastern foedus and allies him). Still to do: Constantine crossing into Gaul, Gildo's Donatist and Moorish flavour, Stilicho's fall (408), now #27.
4. **Unity drivers, not drift.** ✅ The flat drift is gone: Unity now falls only for named causes (Latin West / Greek East court tongues, a regent ruling either half, rival Augusti, different creeds) on top of the existing ones. 395 pace is unchanged at −0.1/month and flattens when Honorius comes of age. Still to add as their features land: lapsed foedera (#1), edicts (#2), usurpers recognised or suppressed (#5), Senior status contested (#2). Maybe a game rule for decay strength.
5. **Usurpation disaster.** For WRE/EAR, driven by a per-region script value (control, estate satisfaction, legitimacy, prestige, war record, distance from capital, debt). Releases a usurper country; Suppress Usurper CB; the War of the Augusti CB serves as Roman civil war. A usurper may petition to join the IO as a third Augustus.

## Tier 2: the decay engine

6. **Roman Authority law** with four tiers (Supreme Imperium → Delegated Dominate → Beholden Rule → Titular Majesty). Both empires start at tier 2. A military-aristocracy estate grows with foederati and non-Roman regiments; at high power and low authority, a "Rule of the Patrician" disaster or situation. Estate privileges are the concessions.
7. **Arian kings over Nicene Romans.** Minority-policy law (Accepted / Tolerated / Unwelcomed, the last unlocking an expel action). A "Germanic Overlords" reform or privilege: more tax from Roman pops, levies from the ruler's own culture.
8. **Church councils.** A situation with voters and a resolution, cloned from vanilla `council_of_trent`. The outcome remaps pop religion by region; seat-holders choose between paying to stay in communion and breaking away. Near our start: Carthage 411 (Donatists), Ephesus 431 (Nestorius), then Chalcedon 451.
9. **Romanization and divergence.** A movement spreading regional Roman cultures while a Roman-group country holds the land; an "Assimilate a Province" action; a late dated event splits Romano-cultures into Romance successors. Makes losing land costly.

### Why the provincials stopped fighting

The West fell partly because its own people no longer thought it worth defending. The sources: Salvian of Marseille (*De gubernatione Dei*, 440s: Romans flee to the barbarians and the Bagaudae to escape the tax collector), Priscus's Greek merchant among the Huns (449), and Valentinian III's *Novel 9* (440). Not Gibbon's "degenerate Romans": each cause below is named and visible, and each has a reform. Beware two popular claims: late veterans still got land and tax exemptions (CTh 7.20), and gladiatorial games ended in both halves around 400, so neither explains the East's survival. Built as separate PRs; 20 and 21 are the causes that feed 17 and 18.

16. **The Right to Bear Arms.** A West law: *Arms Forbidden* at start (small local levies, poorly armed rebels) or *Provincials Armed*, unlocked by crisis as Valentinian III's *Novel 9* did against Vandal raids in 440 (more levies and local defence, stronger risings). Pairs with Patrocinium in explaining why the West cannot raise men.
17. **The Bagaudae.** Where Roman peasants are angry (heavy tax, low satisfaction, Senatorial Immunities in force), a rising in Armorica, the Alps or Tarraconensis releases a Bagaudae rebel country, on the pattern of Constantine III's Britain (#3). Holding them costs troops; ignoring them costs control. The sister of the usurpation disaster (#5).
18. **Fleeing to the barbarians.** Roman pops beside foederati and barbarian kingdoms drift to them ("a lighter yoke"), the more so the worse the West's taxes and justice. A slow cost to the burdens, and it Romanises the kingdoms (#9). Needs the migration test above. ✅ First piece (`on_action/tfe_defectors.txt`): a migrating host that takes a Roman town wins men from it. Every regiment regains 5% of full strength (+2.5% for each of the West's burdens in force), Rome loses manpower in step, and the town is marked Fled to the Host (less growth) for 10 years, so it gives men only once a decade.
19. **Faith in the generals.** A tracked value for each empire, like Unity: defeats and cowardly commanders lower it, victories raise it. Low faith shrinks levies and makes risings (#17) likelier.
20. **Two kinds of justice.** The late-Roman split between *honestiores* and *humiliores*, who faced different penalties for the same crime, as a legal-code law: nobles content, peasants resentful, feeding #17 and #18. The reform is Valentinian I's *defensor civitatis* (368), created to protect the poor from the powerful.
21. **The tax collector and the sale of offices.** *Suffragium* as a named modifier: part of the West's tax never reaches the Fisc. Reformed as Anastasius later did in the East.
22. **The East reforms, the West does not.** The East starts with lighter versions of 16–21 and an easier path out of them, so the halves diverge as they did. Tuning across 16–21, done last.

## Tier 3: theatre set pieces

10. **The Hunnic storm.** A situation cloned from `rise_of_timur`; a tribute IO (Tatar Yoke pattern) for subject Goths, Alans and Gepids; a break-up after Attila. The Huns push hosts into Start Migration. ✅ v1 (`situations/tfe_hunnic_storm.txt`, plan in `docs/plans/2026-09-29-hunnic-storm.md`): the Stirring, the Scourge, the Reckoning; the Hunnic Yoke with its tribute; Submit to the Yoke, Grand Raid (the Hunnic subsidy), Break the Yoke and Stand Against the Scourge. ✅ Slave-taking (`on_action/tfe_hunnic_raiders.txt`): Hunnic armies take a share of the peasants and labourers of every land they occupy and drive them to their capital as slaves. Still to do: terror, Hunnic mercenaries, successor states after Nedao.
11. **Rome and Persia.** A phased rivalry situation (Contention → Cold War → Total War), a once-per-ruler Great War CB, Persian noble houses as estates (the Seven Houses).
12. **Restoration and fun formables.** Reunite the Empire when the IO disbands, regional restorations, and at least one deliberately silly one (TFE has a Pirate Empire of Illyria). Adapt vanilla ROM_f / BYZ_f.
13. **The limes.** Frontier location modifiers or buildings on the Rhine, Danube and eastern frontier that decay unless maintained. ✅ v1 in the West and on the Danube (`tfe_frontier.txt`). ✅ The East too: the Persian front as it stood after 363 (Amida, Martyropolis, Constantina, Resaina, Callinicum, Circesium, Satala; Osrhoene and Mesopotamia given back to Rome in `tools/overrides/80_mesopotamia.txt`) and the desert limes (Palmyra, Bostra, Charach, Aila). Still to do: decay into a ruined tier as the Great Wall of China has, and letting a strong emperor restore them.
14. **Great events and ages.** Justinianic Plague copied from the Black Death situation and disease. Replace `age/00_default.txt` (e.g. 395 / ~527 / ~600) so age-gated content works. Low priority until campaigns reach the 6th century.
15. **Presentation.** Latinised names, loading-screen quotes, dated flavour for holy sites. Cheap and high-impact.
23. **The Silk Road Exchange.** Vanilla's Columbian Exchange retooled (`situations/columbian_exchange.txt` and `generic_actions/columbian_exchange.txt`: a situation plus a paid action that plants a New World good in one of your locations once it reaches your market). Ours spreads what only the East grew in 395, each good unlocked by a named cause: silk once the monks smuggle the silkworm to Constantinople (c. 552), and later the Arab farming revolution's sugar, rice, cotton and saffron. The goods sweep above removed exactly these from the Roman world, so the West's catching up is something the player does and sees. Check first whether `has_origin_in_new_world` has a usable counterpart, or replace it with a scripted trigger listing the goods.
24. **Rise of the Alchon Huns.** A situation for the mid-5th century (Kidarites first, Alchon by c. 450-500), cloned from the same `rise_of_timur` pattern as item 10 but pressing on the Gupta Empire through the northwest (Gandhara, Punjab). No single scripted invasion: it escalates in phases (raids on the frontier → a standing host in Gandhara → sustained conquest pressure), each phase driven by script values (Huna strength, Gupta frontier defence) so the AI hosts actually move and fight. Success takes Gupta northwestern locations, and the loss drains Gupta treasury, prestige and regional control toward the later fragmentation, which it can hasten but never guarantees: a strong Gupta ruler holds. Reuses the slave-taking mechanic from item 10's Hunnic storm work. Needs Gupta, Kidarite and Alchon tags at start (or spawned when the situation opens).
25. **Gildo's revolt.** ✅ v1 (`events/tfe_gildo.txt`, `on_action/tfe_gildo.txt`). Grows the one-shot Gildo event of #3 into an earned rebellion, as an event chain with no situation of its own (the Huns and the Decline of the West, #6, are the only big ones running). `var:tfe_gildo_progress` on the West rises monthly by `tfe_gildo_monthly_rise` (a base, plus the Eastern legions kept in Italy, a Roman Unity under 50 and a Donatist Numidia); left alone it reaches 100 in about three years. Three warnings at 35, 65 and 90 each offer a way to slow it: an inspector, a title or garrisons, and suspending or enforcing the edicts against the Donatists (the last can also speed it). At 100, or after 14 August 397 as the fallback, a flavour event ("Gildo at Cirta") leads into the declaration (`tfe_opening.3`). He rises with the whole diocese of Africa, as in 397 (`tfe_gildo_base_land`: `tunis_area`, `tripolitania_area`, `atlas_high_plateau`, `algiers_area` and the Mauri's Laghouat), with Carthage as capital (Cherchell or Cirta if the West holds it). Tingitana belonged to the diocese of Spain and stays with the West. Earlier he took Caesariensis and Numidia on the day and won the grain coast and Tripolitania town by town over two years; MAZZO313 had him take it all at once (2026-10-02), and `tfe_gildo.6` now only checks the East's homage and his seat. He rises as a vanilla revolt (`start_revolt`), and `tfe_opening.7` makes the revolter his kingdom in the same moment (a day's wait showed the revolt's patchwork of rebel countries for that day), so at full war score the war screen's Annex Revolter takes Africa back whole with no antagonism. A neighbour who backs the revolt (the Mauri, whose capital sits in Laghouat) is sent home, because a backer leads the rebel side and turns Annex Revolter into a white peace. The East's vassal offer now takes effect only once his war with the West is over: a vassal cannot be annexed, and becoming one ends the revolt war. There is no "the war can wait" option any more, since a revolt starts the war itself. The usurper CBs stay as in #3. Vanilla's `nobles_estate` rebel was tried as the progress bar and dropped: a rebel made from script has no pops behind it and is cleaned up within weeks, and its progress cannot be held there. The Roman Authority tier is not read yet, since #6 does not exist; add it to `tfe_gildo_monthly_rise` when it does. `tfe_gildo_revolt_fires` is the one trigger left for the Decline of the West situation to read.
26. **The Decline of the West.** 🚧 Phase 1 (`situations/tfe_decline_of_the_west.txt`): a situation from day one for WRE, EAR, Gildo's Africa and the migrating peoples of Germania and Dacia (`tfe_is_migrator`). Phase 1, The Frontiers Give Way, has two tracks: Gildo's revolt, which runs on its own (#25) and is only shown here, and the Migrators, whose Migrate button (formerly Start Migration in the Form New Country panel) now sits on the situation's panel, paced so the AI peoples do not all leave at once. Rome answers with Hospitalitas (settle a host on a Roman area as foederati; the host usually refuses unless beaten or offered its people's historical land, and a weak Rome (losing its wars, or outmatched by the host) is refused the more; the East gives only Balkan land, and neither Rome may buy off a host at war with the other) and Man the Limes (garrison a frontier stretch for 10 years; no people beyond it may take the road meanwhile). Still to do: later phases; more ways for Rome to answer: Buy Them Off (pay a host to turn aside: a truce with Rome and a CB on another tribe), Set Barbarian against Barbarian (pay one people to fight another, as Stilicho used Uldin against Radagaisus in 406), Appeal to the East (the West spends Unity for Eastern gold or troops, and the East chooses), Settle Laeti (a small band settled on deserted land as military colonists: their pops and flat manpower), and Arm the Provincials (#16).
27. **Stilicho's Glory.** ✅ v1 (`script/defs_stilicho.py`, `script/stilicho_events.py`; spec `docs/specs/2026-09-30-stilichos-glory-design.md`). A bar in the Decline of the West panel, 0 to 100 from 50 with no drift, moved by Stilicho's own battles, migration wars, lost cores, Hospitalitas, Gildo and the Huns. Its tiers make or break the West's morale against Debased Currency. His regency is extended to 408. At 90+ (50% a year), at 100, or in 408 at the latest, Honorius turns on him. Stand with Stilicho (Glory 70+) and he rises against Honorius as a vanilla revolt in Gaul (and Hispania at 90+), with the player following him; Britain and Africa break away. Taking Honorius's capital wins everything, dissolves the Imperium Romanum, and lets the player execute Honorius or exile him to the East's court; being annexed as a revolter is game over. Stand with the Emperor, or lose Stilicho, and Olympius's regency, lost stability and a hollow army begin the collapse, softened by the East's goodwill. The AI always stands with the Emperor, so an AI West falls about nine games in ten. Builds on Gildo's revolt (#25). Stilicho's West (`STILI`) counts as a Western Rome for the barbarians (`tfe_is_western_rome`). A big revolt can come out landless, so the rising marks its revolter and the crowning hands it the land. Still to do: the losing path (Stilicho annexed back into the West) is untested in game.
28. **Honorius after the fall.** What the West becomes once it collapses without Stilicho: a rump state around Ravenna that a player can claw back from. Losing should be fun.

## Constraints to remember

- EU5 ages start in 1342 by default; at 395 everything sits in the first age until the ages file is replaced.
- Vanilla's Byzantine Roman content (bureaucracies, disasters, Restore Roman Borders) is behind the Fate of the Phoenix DLC. Rebuild it, don't depend on it.
- Many vanilla situations and disasters are tied to 1337 countries; stub them out.
- Migration logic, battle casualties and map modes are hardcoded. Society of Pops countries are not playable.
