# Steal the Fallen Eagle's decay, not its meters

The Fallen Eagle (TFE) is a CK3-native total conversion, first published in September 2020 and now at roughly 160k subscribers. It rests on five pillars: a researched Late Antique map with its cultures and faiths; Roman-specific government systems; named historical characters with opening dilemmas; a black-and-gold Late Roman look with its own paper map and soundtrack; and historicity the player can tune through game rules. Its central promise is "save Western Rome, or watch it fall and reclaim it later" ([Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/?id=2243307127); [TFE on X](https://x.com/FallenEagleCK3/status/1554061485072056321)). The most important design lesson comes from its own history. TFE spent 2021–2025 patching and softening an abstract empire-wide "Imperial Competency" collapse meter, then **declared it legacy in the August 2026 beta**. In its place came decay the player can see on the map: a four-tier Roman Authority ladder, a loyalty score per diocese that makes regions defect or crown their own general, regent-strongmen who capture the court, and dioceses that collapse below 30% control ([Beta changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/3515718688)). TFE's 395 bookmark, "A Divided Empire", is the direct ancestor of our start. It puts both Augusti in the hands of regents (Stilicho strongly, Rufinus weakly), binds Alaric by a foedus sworn to the dead Theodosius and then sets him loose, and scripts the British usurpers who lead to Constantine III. Our mod already has the right skeleton for most of this: 395 countries, Roman and Greek culture groups, Nicene, Arian, Donatist and pagan faiths, an Imperium Romanum international organization with a Unity bar, army-based migratory hosts and a starting economy. The best adaptations therefore extend what exists. Unity should get concrete drivers and IO statuses and edicts. Foederati should become a subject type whose loyalty lapses when a ruler dies. Usurpation should be a regional disaster. Doctrine should be settled through a council situation. What we should not copy is any parallel meter that EU5's estates, laws and situations already provide.

## Five pillars behind one promise: save Rome or reclaim it

TFE describes itself as a project that "seeks to faithfully recreate the historical, cultural, and religious developments" of Europe, Africa and Asia as they passed from Classical Antiquity into the medieval era. It frames that as "a detailed and actively-researched" world "filled with historical cultures and religions, new governments, new activities and decisions" ([Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/?id=2243307127)). Around that core the team built **named historical figures** (Iulianus, Stilicho, Constantinus III, Alaric, Odoacer, Nepos, Theodoric, Belisarius, Theodora, Khosrau) and date-specific content such as Alaric's road to the Sack of Rome and Justinian's reconquests. It also built **a full aesthetic identity**: a black-gold UI drawing on Late Roman art, an original soundtrack by George Hammond, new 3D clothing, armour and urban sprawl, Latinised names and a new map font ([Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/?id=2243307127)). The paper map was popular enough to ship on its own for vanilla CK3, and players compare it to Total War: Attila, "especially since Latin remained the lingua franca" ([Workshop 2449634219](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2449634219)). The fifth pillar is **historicity the player controls**. Game rules can disable imperial competence "for more casual games", set migration strength, disable struggles, and split "larp" into Larp, Fictional Larp and Dead Cultures ([Change notes p3](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2243307127?p=3); [p5](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2243307127?p=5); [Changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2243307127)).

TFE puts its accuracy into the setup and its fun into divergence, and that fits our own "fun over accuracy" stance. It rewards alt-history on purpose. Frankish, Iberian and Brythonic rulers can restore Rome. A Romano-Persian struggle can end in "Supplant the Romans and Ērānian". There is a "Pirate Empire of Illyria" formable, added "because why not lol" ([Change notes p3](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2243307127?p=3)). Content ships as **regionally themed named updates**: "House of Sassan" (Jan 2023), "Reign of the Apostate" (Mar 2023, the 361 start) and "Imperial College" (the current CK3 1.19 / All Under Heaven release). Each one bundles a bookmark, a struggle and a batch of decisions ([Change notes p3](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2243307127?p=3); [Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/?id=2243307127)). That is the same batch-by-region rhythm our mod already follows.

Players received it warmly: **6,117 ratings, 159,828 subscribers, 84 dependent items and 7,053 collections** as of September 2026. One submod author wrote that "none have brought me as much enjoyment as The Fallen Eagle" ([Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/?id=2243307127); [Flavor Pack](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/?id=2887285681)). The criticism clusters around fragility rather than design. Decisions and struggles depend on TFE's own county and duchy layout, so map mods break it. Mods that overwrite the HUD remove its custom tabs. Users report crashes, and ecosystem mods fill up with "Update?" comments after every CK3 patch ([Extended Collection discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/discussion/2929347724/3761104682790389098)). The developers' own fixes point to where balance hurt: migration spawn armies were made non-inheritable and shrunk, and the imperial competence meter was repeatedly eased before it was retired ([Change notes p3](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2243307127?p=3); [p5](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2243307127?p=5)). One aesthetic dissenter wants TFE to "drop the Total War Attila aesthetics" ([WtWSMS comments](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2858562094)). Reddit, the CK3 wiki and TFE's own comment section could not be reached. The evidence on *which systems players find fun* is therefore thin, and the design judgments below lean on the developers' own changes rather than player testimony.

Two points of history need correcting. I found no CK2 version of TFE: the CK2-lineage Late Antiquity mod is *When the World Stopped Making Sense*, which has its own CK3 port ([Workshop 2858562094](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2858562094)). The official starts are **361, 395, 476 and 532**, with 632 "TBD" ([Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/?id=2243307127)). The installed files confirm a `bm_532_reconquest` bookmark dated 532.2.1 ([00_bookmarks.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/bookmarks/bookmarks/00_bookmarks.txt)). 395 was the original launch era, and it is the start YouTubers keep choosing: "Can Stilicho Save Rome?", AI timelapses from 395, "Restoring The Western ROMAN EMPIRE … Was INCREDIBLE" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bcwC7aIJUsE); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jpphcabbk-0); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YHRzpdyLG7A)).

## Rome decays through authority tiers, diocese loyalty and captured courts

Almost every Roman system in the current build re-purposes a vanilla CK3 1.19 feature (the administrative government, the treasury, confederations, diarchies, struggles, task contracts and the Liberty faction) and layers TFE's own scripted logic on top ([Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2243307127)). That is itself a lesson for us: build on the host engine's native systems.

**The Roman Imperial government** is administrative and funded by a treasury. Mercenaries cost four times the normal price, diplomatic range is ten times normal, and 95% of vassal income flows to the top liege ([imperial_government.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/governments/imperial_government.txt)). Sibling governments cover the rest of the Roman world. `roman_government` is for Romans outside an intact empire, including those under barbarian kings. `romanesque_government` drifts to Feudal once Feudalization is discovered. `manorial_government` models the villae. Persia, the Caliphate, the Gupta and the Oasis city-states each get their own government ([Mod files](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2243307127)).

**Roman Authority** is the spine. It is a four-tier realm law: Titular Majesty, Beholden Rule, Delegated Dominate and Supreme Imperium ([00_00_TFE_roman_authority_laws.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/laws/00_00_TFE_roman_authority_laws.txt)). Raising a tier costs prestige and influence and gives every direct vassal −20 opinion for five years; lowering one is free. Each tier decides what the emperor may do:

- revoke titles and ban wars between vassals;
- designate an heir;
- use the "Legitimize Usurper" interaction and the Roman Civil War casus belli, both at tier 2 and above;
- keep a strongman regent, which is possible below tier 3 and impossible at it.

Each change also re-syncs a **Civil-Military Divide** law. At tiers 2–3 generals may hold no civilian title. At tier 1 they may hold counties and duchies, and at tier 0 kingdoms as well; the flavour text cites Stilicho, Aetius and Ricimer.

The engine that pushes authority down is the key design. The vanilla Liberty faction's power threshold falls by 0.5 points for every percent of the military-command vassals who are **not Roman or Greek**, and each "barbarized" officer adds +30 to his join score. The code comment says a fully barbarized officer corps at neutral opinion will drag the emperor down one tier ([00_factions.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/factions/00_factions.txt)). At low authority, **Militarize Administration** lets a dux seize his duchy, or a provincial Magister Militum his whole diocese. The capital is exempt, and the province returns to civil rule when the general dies ([Mod files](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2243307127)).

**Offices** follow the Notitia Dignitatum. On the civil side run Praetorian Prefects over four prefectures, Vicars over dioceses, Praesides over provinces and Curatores over counties. On the military side are **29 static landless commands**: field armies under a Comes in the West or a Magister Militum in the East, and border armies under Duces. Eight palatine ministers complete the court. A dux gives his duchy +50% raid time, +25% garrison and +1 fort level ([Beta changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/3515718688)). Frontier **limes** forts are seeded per bookmark and decay over 25-year steps (reinforced → standard → neglected → removed) unless diocese projects maintain them ([TFE_limes_seeding_effects.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/scripted_effects/TFE_limes_seeding_effects.txt)).

**The Imperial College** is a repurposed confederation holding the Western, Eastern and any newly acclaimed Augusti ([TFE_roman_imperial_college.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/confederation_types/TFE_roman_imperial_college.txt)). Its rules:

- The longest-reigning member is **Senior Augustus**.
- Members may ally freely but may fight each other only through the Suppress Usurper or Roman Civil War casus belli.
- The college dissolves when one member remains, and the survivor's title becomes "Roman Empire".
- Only the Senior may proclaim **Imperial Edicts**. Each costs 100 influence and binds every member for five years.

There are five edicts, each with a cost attached to its benefit:

| Edict | Benefit | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Decretum de Fide | faith unity | penalties with other faiths |
| Codificatio Legum | control, tax, legitimacy | vassal opinion |
| Restitutio Cursus Publici | travel speed, vassal opinion | vassal tax |
| Lex de Agris Desertis | development, tax | vassal opinion |
| Edictum de Annona | supply | development growth |

**Diarchies** model the regents who ruled the emperors. A Caesar diarchy handles heirs. A seven-tier **Patriciate** entrenched regency models Stilicho and Ricimer: its swing settles at an equilibrium set by authority, it scores candidates on their office and the emperor's trust, and it favours the lowborn "because they cannot threaten the throne" ([Beta changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/3515718688); [Mod files](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2243307127)).

**Diocese loyalty** is the most portable piece. It is a deterministic score, cached quarterly, built from:

- the vicar's opinion;
- the ruler's prestige and legitimacy;
- +10 for the Senior Augustus, −20 for a usurper;
- war record over the last five years, capped at +20/−50;
- the opinions of the praesides, the field general and the border duces;
- squared distance from the capital, capped at −30;
- a negative treasury;
- dread.

The score is shown in six bands, from Loyal (≥ +30) to Seething (below −90). Below −60 a diocese either **defects to a hostile neighbouring Augustus**, or **acclaims its field general** if he is competent enough (martial, prowess, commander traits and prestige) and loyal enough to it ([TFE_acclamation_values.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/script_values/TFE_acclamation_values.txt); [tfe_diocese_cascade_events.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/events/tfe_diocese_cascade_events.txt)). The designer's stated target is **about one defection per ten years per Augustus**, held there by 10-year immunity timers and 5-year cooldowns. That pacing discipline is worth copying outright. A player can also force the issue with the decision "Claim the Purple". It requires a field-army command and 1000 gold, costs 500 prestige and 100 influence, and spawns a "[Diocese] Roman Empire" with the diocese's officials and commands.

The fall, and the way back, run on the map. Losing the capital offers a court relocation or "The Empire falls". A diocese holding under 30% of its counties dissolves into independent counties. Restoration is staged: "Create Diocese", then "Reunite the Western/Eastern Roman Empire", then "Declare Hegemony over the Roman World", then "Proclaim the Empire Restored" (Restitutor Orbis, which needs all 15 dioceses). "Split the Roman Empire" lets a sole emperor partition the realm again between two heirs ([Mod files](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2243307127)).

The system TFE threw away matters as much as the ones it kept. Imperial Competency had three ±150/month meters, a collapse state and an "Ungarrisoned Borders" penalty. It arrived in October 2021, got a disable rule in January 2022, was rebalanced "easier" twice in 2025, and was made legacy in August 2026 ([Changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2243307127); [Beta changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/3515718688)). We can reasonably infer that players experienced the global meter as opaque and punishing, while the replacement makes collapse **legible on the map and attributable to people**: this general, this diocese, this regent. Our Unity bar is exactly that kind of global meter. It will stay healthy only if its drivers remain concrete, visible and few.

## The 395 bookmark opens with both courts in regents' hands

TFE's 395.1.17 group has three bookmarks. The main one, **"A Divided Empire"**, frames the day as Theodosius's death, with the two sons' "youth and inexperience becoming mediums of power for the politicians and generals" at Mediolanum and Constantinople. Hunnic incursions "spur Germanic migrations", "usurpers stake their claims", and it asks whether you will "strengthen or unwind the threads that bind the Roman world together" ([bm_395_empire_l_english.yml](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/localization/english/replace/bookmark/bm_395_empire_l_english.yml)). The featured characters, with their difficulty ratings, are:

| Character | Holds | Faith, culture | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Honorius | Western Empire (with Stilicho) | Nicene, Roman | very hard |
| Arcadius | Eastern Empire (with Eudoxia) | Nicene, Roman | hard |
| Alaric | tribal Visigoths | Arian, Gothic | easy |
| Faramund | Franks | Central pagan, Salian | hard |
| Constantinus III | a special county | Nicene, Britano-Roman | very hard |
| Stilicho | magister utriusque militiae (a minister title) | Nicene, Roman | medium |

A Gildo entry for African Roman Nicene Africa is commented out ([00_bookmarks.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/bookmarks/bookmarks/00_bookmarks.txt)). The other two 395 bookmarks are "The House of Sassan" (Bahram IV, Armenia, Axum, Himyar, the Tanukhids and the Lakhmids) and "Omens from the Steppes" (the Rouran and the Sixteen Kingdoms states).

The game-start script turns that framing into mechanics ([TFE_game_start.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/on_action/TFE_game_start.txt)):

- **Both Augusti begin at authority tier 2, Delegated Dominate.** The code comment reads "the Patriciate emerging IS the visible power decay". Julian in 361 starts at tier 3, so the drop is the story.
- **Rufinus** is Arcadius's Patrician at swing 30, the "Patricius Fidelis" tier. **Stilicho** is Honorius's at swing 70, the Praefect tier, "de facto rulership", one step below "Imperator". The West is a regency in all but name; the East is a contested court.
- **Alaric becomes an Eastern foederati tributary on 395.1.17, with his foedus sworn to the late Theodosius.** A scripted "Visigothic Revolt of 395 (Alaric's Rampage)" then launches `cb_gothic_wars` against the holder of Dacia and pulls Eastern vassals in as defenders.
- **Usurpation in Britain** is scripted as the chain Marcus → Gratian → Constantine III (407). Gaul defects if its vicar is loyal to the absent Augustus, under the modifier "Sympathies with the British Acclamation".
- **Limes forts** are seeded at tier 2 on the Limes Germanicus, the western and eastern Danube and the Limes Orientalis, and at tier 1 on the Limes Arabicus ([TFE_limes_seeding_effects.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/scripted_effects/TFE_limes_seeding_effects.txt)).
- **Armies**: the East fields about 15k men in 5 field and 10 border armies, the West about 13.5k in 4 field and 10 border armies ([Beta changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/3515718688)).

Background clocks then run from the same date. The Jovinian heresy event fires at the holder of Rome from 393 ([TFE_christian_events.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/events/religion_events/TFE_christian_events.txt)). A Radagaisus invasion is scripted ([Mod files](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2243307127)). The "Fate of the Slavs" ethnogenesis has a window of 400–450, and the Romanization pulse runs every three years ([TFE_yearly_on_actions.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/on_action/TFE_yearly_on_actions.txt)). The Council of Chalcedon waits for 451.

TFE's 395 therefore does three things that ours does not yet do:

- It makes **day one a crisis of personal bonds**: the treaty was with Theodosius, not with you.
- It makes the **two courts asymmetric**: a strong regent in the West, a weak one in the East.
- It runs **usurpation as a timed, regional threat** rather than a random roll.

Our Imperium Romanum IO already encodes the first layer of the division: Unity starts at 65, drifts down 0.1 a month, is raised by a shared dynasty and a common enemy, and is lowered by resentment, disputed lands, denied military access and civil war ([tfe_roman_empire.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/compatdata/3450310/pfx/drive_c/users/steamuser/Documents/Paradox%20Interactive/Europa%20Universalis%20V/mod/TFE/in_game/common/international_organizations/tfe_roman_empire.txt)). The IO grants military access and automatic defensive calls at 75 and above, allows calls for help at 50 and above, and permits the existing `cb_tfe_war_of_the_augusti` below 50 ([tfe_war_of_the_augusti.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/compatdata/3450310/pfx/drive_c/users/steamuser/Documents/Paradox%20Interactive/Europa%20Universalis%20V/mod/TFE/in_game/common/casus_belli/tfe_war_of_the_augusti.txt)). That is TFE's Imperial College rule ("members fight only through civil-war casus belli") expressed as a threshold. The missing layers are the regents, the foedus and the usurpers.

## Barbarians arrive as treaties, and Romans slowly stop being Roman

**Foederati** in TFE form a special tributary contract. They pay no tribute but must join the suzerain's wars. Each foedus is **a personal oath to one Augustus that lapses when either party dies**. It is renewed by "Petition to Renew the Foedus" or "Strike New Foedus". It carries two negotiable terms: **Annona Foederatica** (None, Standard or Lavish, paid from the imperial treasury) and **Hospitalitas** (land Denied or Granted) ([Changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2243307127); [Mod files](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2243307127)). A separate client-kingdom contract covers realms beyond the frontier. Its terms are a subsidy, trade rights, supply access and "Imperial Recognition". AI acceptance weighs faith, culture, weakness and distance. Breaking free needs prestige level 2 or legitimacy level 2. Migration flows into this system. When a migrating host invades Roman land, the defender gets three options: **cede the target duchy as foederati land, pay 500 gold, or fight on**. A migrant who accepts the foedus and then wins still settles as a tributary ([Mod files](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2243307127)). This turns barbarian settlement into a negotiation and gives the Roman player a budget choice: gold or land.

**Migration itself** is a once-per-lifetime `germanic_migration_cb`. It is limited to cultures with the migrations tradition and to the era before the early medieval period. It targets duchies, spawns event armies scaled to the phase of the regional struggle, and converts the culture of the counties taken. Siblings exist for Huns, Rouran, Slavs, Brythons and Bolghars ([00_migration_wars.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/casus_belli_types/00_migration_wars.txt)). The spawned stacks were the main balance pain point, trimmed and made non-inheritable across several patches ([Change notes p3](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2243307127?p=3)). Our army-based hosts, with Start Migration, the `cb_tfe_migration` casus belli and settlement on the first land won, already avoid spawned stacks. A host carries its real warband and cannot replenish while landless ([tfe_migratory.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/compatdata/3450310/pfx/drive_c/users/steamuser/Documents/Paradox%20Interactive/Europa%20Universalis%20V/mod/TFE/in_game/common/generic_actions/tfe_migratory.txt)). That is arguably a better design than TFE's. What we lack is the *Roman answer* to a host: the cede, pay or fight choice.

**Culture** follows a two-stage arc. While a Latin-heritage realm holds a region, a three-yearly pulse turns counties into regional Romano-cultures, weighted from Italo-Roman (500) down to Britano-Roman (100). An "Assimilate a Diocese" great project speeds this up through colonists, rhetors and resettlement ([TFE_yearly_on_actions.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/on_action/TFE_yearly_on_actions.txt); [TFE_great_project_types.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/great_projects/types/TFE_great_project_types.txt)). Between 700 and 750 a global event **splits the Romano-cultures into Romance successors**: Gallo-Roman to Occitan and Armorican, Dacio- and Thracio-Roman to Vlach, and so on. It yields Andalusian or Mozarabic where southern Iberia is Muslim. The notification reads "while I still think myself as a proper Roman… I can't help but notice how different my day-to-day life has become" ([tfe_roman_culture_divergence.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/events/tfe_roman_culture_divergence.txt)).

Barbarian peoples get "Fate of X" ethnogenesis splits in date windows (Slavs, Aesti, Goths into Visigoths and Ostrogoths). The player can refuse a split with "we are still one people!" only if he holds **at least 80% of the parent culture** ([tfe_culture_events.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/events/tfe_culture_events.txt)). The **Germanic Overlords** tradition captures Gothic kings over Roman subjects: the locals do "much of the governing" while the barbarians form the military. It raises minority tax contribution by 30% and cuts minority levies by 25% ([TFE_realm_traditions.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/culture/traditions/TFE_realm_traditions.txt)). **Roman Hubris** makes Romans slower to accept other cultures (−25%), and **Bread and Circuses** unlocks grain doles and games. TFE has no single "Roman-ness" meter; identity is spread across heritage, traditions, faith and struggle state. If we want Romanitas to be legible, we must consolidate it ourselves.

## Creeds sleep until history wakes them

TFE models Christianity as one religion with about 30 faiths. Most are **dormant**, blocked by a hidden `unavailable_doctrine` until an event unlocks them ([00_christianity.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/religion/religion_types/00_christianity.txt)). The key faiths:

- **Nicene** has a Pentarchy tenet (Jerusalem, Constantinople, Rome, Alexandria, Antioch as holy sites) and a head styled "Patriarch".
- **Arianism** shares those holy sites but is headless and outside ecumenical tolerance. Its loc says it "remains opposed to the forms of Christianity that cling to the old doctrines".
- **Donatism** swaps Antioch for **Carthage** and carries a custom "Pure Priesthood" tenet: the sinful clergy "should be cast aside" ([TFE_religion_core_tenets_l_english.yml](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/localization/english/replace/religion/TFE_religion_core_tenets_l_english.yml)).

The Arian–Nicene tension shows up **indirectly**: through hostility doctrines, the Germanic Overlords tradition and a county-minorities layer. There is no dedicated "Arian king converts" chain.

The set piece is the **Council of Chalcedon**. From 451 a hidden global event deletes Nicene as a choosable faith. It remaps every Nicene county, minority and character **by geographic region** into Chalcedonian, Coptic, Syriac, Armenian Apostolic or Nestorian, preserving minority sizes. Each Pentarchy seat-holder then chooses. "It is what had to be done" costs 200 gold and 50 prestige for 100 piety. ## Every theatre runs on its own clock

Beyond Rome, TFE gives each region its own phased narrative through CK3 **struggles**:

| Struggle | Phases | Endings |
|---|---|---|
| Romano-Ērānian Wars | Contention → Cold War → Total War | Roman or Ērānian victory, regional independence, outsider supplant |
| Fall of Rome (Italy) | Turmoil → Devastation → Rebuilding | Synthesis ("Rome never died… It simply changed"), Incorporation, Restoration, Subjugation |
| Britannia | Migration → Opportunity → Hostility → Conciliation → Compromise | Bretwalda, "Britannia for the Celts", Reclaim Greater Britannia, Détente, Status Quo |

The Romano-Ērānian struggle has proxy wars and a **Great War casus belli** that seizes whole kingdoms but is lost for the ruler's lifetime after a single defeat ([Mod files](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2243307127)). The Italian struggle rewards cross-faith accommodation: privileges, feasts and interfaith alliances ([tfe_italian_struggle.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/struggle/struggles/tfe_italian_struggle.txt); [tfe_struggle_decisions.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/decisions/tfe_struggle_decisions.txt)).

Persia runs the **Seven Great Houses**. Each house has two dials, Favor and Strength, each 0–100. Every point of Favor away from 50 gives stacking realm modifiers (tax, levy reinforcement, cavalry, county opinion), doubled when Strength is 50 or more. Favor at 70 or above, or 30 or below, fires struggle catalysts ([sevenhouses_on_actions.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/on_action/sevenhouses_on_actions.txt)).

Great events are keyed to a date window *and* to whoever holds the key city. The Justinianic Plague strikes the holder of Constantinople between 541 and 549. The "Endless Winter" ash year comes after 535. The Gothic and Vandalic wars are decisions led by the emperor ([Mod files](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2243307127)). Every region also gets at least one **restoration fantasy** (Parthia, Bosporus, Assyria, "Heir of Attila", the Pirate Empire of Illyria) and one **monument** with a building payoff (the Ma'rib Dam, the Gorgan Wall, Taq-Kasra). The Third Temple chain is a "defy prophecy" megaproject. Recurring Fire!, Sabotage! and Earthquake! events ask the player each time whether to sink more money or quit ([Change notes p3](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2243307127?p=3); [western_roman_events.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/events/western_roman_events.txt)).

## EU5 has the parts, but it trades characters for countries and estates

CK3's systems hang on characters: vassals, opinion, councils, diarchies. EU5's hang on **countries, estates, pops and international organizations**, so each TFE idea has to be re-anchored rather than ported. The good news is that almost every relevant EU5 system is a script database with a readme ([game common folder](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/common/Europa%20Universalis%20V/game/in_game/common/)):

- **International organizations**, with variables (how our `tfe_unity` works), special statuses with auto-bestow and auto-dismiss triggers, payments, IO laws and a leader election through `leadership_election_resolution`. The HRE has all of these.
- **Resolutions**, voted by IO members or situation voters. The readme says a resolution can be built "entirely in script… no need for coders".
- **Situations**, with a `voters` list. The Council of Trent is a working template for a church council.
- **Disasters**, per country. `byzantine_succession_crisis` and `decline_of_empire` are templates.
- **Subject types**, bindable to a country type.
- **Laws and policies**; the Tribal Migration law's `permanent_settlement` policy switches a country to the settled type after five years.
- **Estates and privileges**, 261 privileges in total, including tribal ones.
- **Movements**, which spread culture or religion through pops like a contagion.
- **Diseases**, with an SIR model; the Black Death is a disease wrapped in a situation.
- **Formables**, with a `historical`/`plausible`/`fantasy` rule that mirrors TFE's larp game rules.
- **Generic actions**, EU5's decisions and buttons.
- **Cabinet actions** such as `promote_culture`, `settle_tribesmen` and `expel_people`.

Sources: [situations readme](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/common/Europa%20Universalis%20V/game/in_game/common/situations/readme.txt); [international_organizations readme](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/common/Europa%20Universalis%20V/game/in_game/common/international_organizations/readme.txt); [resolutions readme](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/common/Europa%20Universalis%20V/game/in_game/common/resolutions/readme.txt); [00_tribes.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/common/Europa%20Universalis%20V/game/in_game/common/laws/00_tribes.txt).

Three templates map almost one to one:

- The **Rise of Timur** situation gives a named conqueror an area preference and an expansionist AI personality: a Hunnic arc ready to clone.
- The **Tatar Yoke** IO models tribute flowing from subjects to a collector to a leader: Attila's tribute empire.
- The **Ilkhanate** IO passes leadership to the last claimant standing: the struggle after Attila.

Sources: [rise_of_timur.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/common/Europa%20Universalis%20V/game/in_game/common/situations/rise_of_timur.txt); [tatar_yoke.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/common/Europa%20Universalis%20V/game/in_game/common/international_organizations/tatar_yoke.txt).

The constraints are real, but they are known. **Migration and battle-casualty logic and map modes are hardcoded** ([EU5 Wiki: Modding](https://eu5.paradoxwikis.com/Modding)). We can call `add_migration`, `change_country_type` and cabinet actions, but we cannot change how pops choose destinations. **Ages are keyed to absolute years** (1, then 1342, 1437, …), so at 395 the whole game sits in the first age for nine centuries unless we replace `age/00_default.txt` ([age/00_default.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/common/Europa%20Universalis%20V/game/in_game/common/age/00_default.txt)). The vanilla Byzantine Roman flavour (`cb_restore_roman_borders`, the Latinization/Hellenization societal value, the Roman culture movement, the Byzantine bureaucracies) is **gated behind the Fate of the Phoenix DLC** and keyed to the BYZ tag, so a free mod must re-implement it rather than call it. Vanilla situations and disasters keyed to 1337 tags either never fire or throw errors.

Three questions the research could not answer bear directly on the shortlist:

- whether **army-based countries can be subjects or IO members** (this decides whether a migrating host can hold a foedus before it settles);
- whether **religions can be enabled mid-game**, which TFE's dormant-faith trick needs;
- whether a new **government type** can be added by script.

Each needs a quick in-game test before we commit.

The translation rule that follows is this. **Where CK3 TFE used a character, EU5 should use a country, an estate or an IO status.** Where TFE used a per-vassal opinion score, EU5 should use location control, estate satisfaction and a script value per region. Where TFE used a struggle, EU5 should use a situation.

## Conclusion

The deepest thing TFE teaches is not any single mechanic. It is how it paced collapse. It abandoned a global doom meter because players could not read it, and it replaced it with decay that has a face: Stilicho's grip, Britain's general, Alaric's lapsed oath. It also capped the chaos at roughly one defection per decade per emperor. Our Unity bar is at risk of becoming the meter TFE retired, unless its movement is traced to named, mappable causes and its thresholds unlock visible events rather than silent penalties. The second lesson is that TFE's best 395 content is *relational*. The foedus dies with Theodosius, the regents own the courts, and the usurpers are timed to the West's weakness. EU5 can express these relations through subjects, estates and IO statuses more natively than CK3 ever could through character opinion.

Our mod also has room to *beat* TFE on its known weak points. Hosts that carry their real warband avoid the spawned-stack balance problem TFE kept patching. Owning our map layer outright avoids the compatibility fragility its users complain about. EU5's pops already provide the minorities system that TFE had to bolt on. The order of work that follows builds outward from what is already in the files: the IO, the civil-war casus belli, the migration pipeline. Parallel systems come last.
