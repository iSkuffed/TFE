## Q1. Christian schisms and doctrine (Nicene/Chalcedonian, Arian, Miaphysite, Nestorian, Donatist; councils, heresy, pentarchy, Pope vs Constantinople, Arian rulers over Nicene subjects)

### Takeaway
TFE models Christianity as one religion with about 30 faiths. Most are **dormant** until history "unlocks" them: a hidden `unavailable_doctrine` blocks conversion to them. The main set piece is a scripted **Council of Chalcedon (451)**. It deletes "Nicene" as a faith and redistributes every Nicene county, minority and character **by geographic region** into Chalcedonian, Coptic (Miaphysite), Syriac, Armenian Apostolic or Nestorian. Rulers who hold a council seat get to accept the council or break with it.

Arianism is a standing rival faith with no religious head. How Arian barbarian elites rule over Nicene Roman subjects is handled through **culture** (the "Germanic Overlords" tradition) and a county-level **religious minorities** layer, not through a special religious mechanic.

### Inferences
- The reusable pattern is **"faith switched off until an event", then a region-keyed mass conversion**. That is cheap to build: one flag per faith, plus one region→faith lookup table applied to provinces, minorities and characters together. For EU5 this maps to hidden religions enabled by a scripted event, followed by a location-region remap of pop religion.
- The Chalcedon seat-holder choice is a small but meaningful fork: pay gold and prestige to stay in communion, or take prestige with excommunication and lose ecumenical tolerance. The AI almost always accepts, so breaking away is effectively the player's choice.
- The Arian/Nicene tension mostly shows up **indirectly**: hostility levels, the lack of an Arian head, minority tax/levy trade-offs and the Italian struggle catalysts. There is no dedicated "Arian king converts to Nicene" event chain in the files I inspected.

### Gaps
- I found no event that spawns Miaphysite/Chalcedonian divisions after 451 (Henotikon, Acacian schism, Three Chapters) and no Second Council of Constantinople. None found by grep for "council/synod/schism".
- I did not verify the exact holy-site bonuses or the exact meaning of the Pentarchy tenet (vanilla CK3 content).
- Player opinion on the Chalcedon event: none retrievable (web blocked).

## Q2. Paganism: Hellenic/Roman survival, Germanic, Slavic, Celtic, Arabian; reformation, conversion pressure, syncretism

### Takeaway
Pagan religions are split finely by region and period: 4 Hellenic faiths, 5 Celtic, 4 Germanic, about 10 Semitic/Arabian, and 6 Egyptian/Kushite. Syncretism is the main mechanic. A custom **Hellenic Syncretism** tenet and heritage doctrines control which faiths see each other as Righteous or Astray.

Hellenic faiths start **reformed**, with the emperor as religious head (Imperial head, lay clergy). Most Germanic, Celtic and Arabian faiths start **unreformed**. The Celtic faiths can restore a druidic priesthood by decision. Several small ritual decisions give pagan play texture.

## Q4. Culture: Roman/Latin vs Greek vs barbarian, Romanization/Hellenization, hybridization/divergence, ethnogenesis, unique traditions

### Takeaway
Culture works as a **Romanization → divergence** lifecycle:
1. Random 3-yearly events turn counties into regional **Romano-cultures** (Gallo-, Hispano-, Italo-, Afro-, Britano-Roman, and others). These only fire while the realm or its liege is of Latin heritage, and only before the divergence.
2. From about 700–750, a global event **splits the Romano-cultures into Romance successors** (Castilian, Catalan, Occitan, Italian, Vlach, Romansh, and others). Where the region is Muslim, it produces Mozarabic/Andalusian instead.

Barbarian peoples go through scripted **"Fate of X" ethnogenesis splits** (Slavs, Aesti, Antes, Wends, Sclaveni, Tiele, Gothones, Bavarians, Goths→Visigoths/Ostrogoths), plus random melting-pot events (Suebi, Anglo-Saxons). Hybrid cultures get **"X-Roman" names** when Latin heritage mixes with others. The **Italian "Fall of Rome" struggle** asks "what it means to be Roman" and can end in a Roman–Germanic *Synthesis*.

### Inferences
- The cultural arc is essentially: **spread Latinity while the Empire lives → fragment it into Romance cultures once it is gone**. It makes a clean two-stage EU5 design: a pop-culture assimilation toward a *regional* Roman culture, followed by a dated or conditional "Romance split" whose outcome depends on who rules (Christian or Muslim).
- Letting the player refuse a split if they hold ≥80% of the culture is a good player-agency hook: unity is earned by holding territory.
- Romanization is heritage-gated (only Latin-heritage realms or vassals of one). The Empire therefore has to *keep* territory to keep Romanizing, which makes collapse costly.

## Q5. Legitimacy / "Roman-ness" / civilizational identity tied to culture or religion

### Takeaway
"Roman-ness" is carried by **heritage_latin** (which gates Romanization), the **Roman Hubris / Roman Legacy** traditions, the **Imperial Faith / Imperial Divinity** innovations, the Hellenic *imperial head* doctrine (the emperor as pontifex), and the struggle endings that decide whether Rome continues through synthesis or conquest. Official Faith links religion to the Roman state, and the Britannia struggle's Roman ending requires cultural and religious homogeneity.

## Cross-cutting system: county religious and cultural minorities

### Takeaway
TFE ships an integrated **minorities** system: files prefixed `sea_`, origin/author not determined. Each county can hold **small or large minority faiths and cultures**. They migrate, grow, and can displace the majority. Rulers pick a **minority policy law** (Accepted / Tolerated / Unwelcomed), and the harshest unlocks **expulsion**. Scripted events such as Chalcedon operate on minorities too. For EU5 this maps closely onto pops.

### Cited Findings
- Concept text: "A Minority Faith represents a proportion of the population of a county which follows another faith. Minorities may be small or large. A small minority may grow to become a large minority, and a large minority may grow enough to displace the original faith of the county, which becomes a new minority in that county." The same applies to cultures — [SRC:/localization/english/replace/sea_minority_game_concepts_l_english.yml](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/localization/english/replace/sea_minority_game_concepts_l_english.yml).
- Religious Minority Policy laws:
  - "Other Faiths Accepted": disallows converting county faith.
  - "Tolerated": allows converting county faith.
  - "Unwelcomed": allows conversion of minority faiths and unlocks "Expel Religious Minority".
  - Cultural laws mirror these.
  - Expulsion gives an "Expelled My Faith/Culture" opinion penalty, and the event lets the ruler seize "belongings left behind".
  - [SRC:/localization/english/replace/sea_minority_laws_l_english.yml](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/localization/english/replace/sea_minority_laws_l_english.yml); [SRC:/common/decisions/sea_minority_decisions.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/decisions/sea_minority_decisions.txt).
- Yearly per-county rolls:
  - 50%: county-to-county culture/faith migration. Blocked if the culture has Isolationist or the faith has Communal Identity.
  - 20%: minority culture growth.
  - 20%: minority faith growth.
  - Minority characters spawn every 3 years (large minorities) and every 5 years (small ones).
  - The court chaplain task can convert a minority, and the steward-style task can promote the liege's culture over one.
  - [SRC:/common/on_action/sea_minority_on_actions.txt](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/common/on_action/sea_minority_on_actions.txt); [concepts loc](file:///home/skuffed/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/workshop/content/1158310/2243307127/localization/english/replace/sea_minority_game_concepts_l_english.yml).

### Inferences
- This is the layer that makes "Arian Goths ruling Nicene Romans" actually play out in TFE: a Nicene majority county under an Arian lord can keep an Arian minority, and so on. It fits EU5's native pop model almost exactly. The policy-law trio is a ready-made EU5 law design.

### Gaps
- I could not identify the original source mod or author of the `sea_` minority system.
